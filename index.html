What is

Sakhalin

Sakhalin Island is the largest island in Russia, yet Sakhalin is little-known island in the northwest Pacific, just above Japan. While it isn’t as touristy as Bali or Mexico, it offers raw, unforgettable nature and authentic experiences. It’s wild, diverse, and deeply scenic due to its misty coasts, taiga forests, and magnificent mountains.

For me, Sakhalin is more than just a place; it’s my home. It’s where I grew up, where my family is, and where some of my most cherished memories were made. Every corner of Sakhalin holds a special place in my heart. However, in this travelogue, I want to share my favorite hidden gems that make Sakhalin truly special.

My top 3 favorite places and why I love them as much as I do

Saltwater lagoon

Busse Lake

Don’t let the name fool you. Lake Busse isn’t actually a lake, but a saltwater lagoon in Southern Sakhalin. This hidden gem has a wild, untamed beauty that feels almost otherworldly.

What makes Lake Busse truly exceptional is the way it transforms with the tides. At low tide, massive oyster beds emerge from the water like a secret feast unveiled by the sea. But these aren’t your typical oysters from Canada or France. These are giant Pacific oysters, fresh from the ocean, often so large they can serve as an entire meal on their own. Visitors are welcome to walk right out onto the exposed beds, pick their oysters straight from the lagoon, shuck them open, and taste the ocean in its purest form.

Imagine walking across a field of oysters, each one the size of a full meal. It’s a shellfish lover’s dream, unlike anything else on Earth.

Why I love it

For me, it’s more than just nature’s wonder, it’s a piece of my heart. My dad built a small eco-hotel there, where every summer our family used to gather to fish, relax, and feast on oysters together. The moments I've spent there are some of my most precious memories.

Mountain Air

Gorny Vozdukh

Right in the heart of Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk rises Gorny Vozdukh, a mountain that has always felt like the island’s living, breathing soul. When I lived on Sakhalin, it was my favorite place to hike, bike, and fill my lungs with crisp mountain air. The trails weave through lush valleys, dense forests, and sparkling springs, leading up to a peak where surreal panoramic views stretch endlessly across land and mountains.

In winter, the mountain transforms into Sakhalin’s ski hub, with 10 trails covering more than 23 kilometers. It’s an unbeatable winter sports getaway, just minutes from the city center. And the best part? Night skiing. Under the glow of floodlights, you carve through the snow while the city glitters far below.

Why I love it

My grandma’s house was just a five-minute walk away. After a long day of climbing and riding the funicular back down, I’d head straight to her place, where my grandma was always waiting with warm hugs and abundant homemade cooking.

Historical Treasure

Aniva Lighthouse

Down at the southern tip of Sakhalin, there’s a place that feels straight out of a movie: Aniva Lighthouse. Everyone on the island knows it, and for good reason. Built by the Japanese back in 1939, it was a real feat of engineering at the time. The lighthouse kept watch over these rough waters for decades, but after it shut down in 2006, it was left to the mercy of the ocean, the storms, and curious adventurers who managed to reach it.

These days, Aniva is a mix of ruin and wonder. Inside, it’s crumbling and eerie, like a ghost of its past life. Outside, it’s pure magic, a lonely tower standing tall against the endless Pacific, battered but still defiant.

Why I love it

My dad used to take me out to the lighthouse, and together we would climb up to the top of the lighthouse to observe the breathtaking views of the ocean and nature around. Those trips weren’t just about the destination, they were about the time we shared together. That’s why Aniva will always stay with me: it’s a piece of my life with my dad.

Thank you for reading!

I hope you enjoyed this little tour of my favorite places on Sakhalin Island. If you ever get the chance to visit, I promise you won’t regret it. Until then, I’ll be dreaming of those oysters, mountain air, and the lonely lighthouse.
